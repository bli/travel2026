A visual journey from Shanghai’s cinematic waterfront and historic neighbourhoods to Suzhou’s refined gardens and lantern-lit canals.

Two cities,
two rhythms.

Shanghai moves between grand riverside architecture, neon, shopping streets and futuristic towers. Suzhou slows the pace with carefully composed gardens, whitewashed houses and waterways shaped by centuries of life in the Jiangnan region.

Shanghai Icons

Begin beside the Huangpu River, where historic façades face one of Asia’s most recognisable modern skylines.

Daytime

外滩白天

The Bund by Day

Daylight reveals the Bund’s stately early-20th-century architecture, broad riverside promenade and dramatic contrast with Pudong across the water. Walk slowly and look back at the façades as well as the skyline.

Best: morning or late afternoonImage source ↗
Night

外滩夜景

The Bund at Night

After dark, the historic waterfront glows gold while Lujiazui becomes a wall of colour across the river. The experience is livelier and more theatrical than by day, especially from the riverside promenade.

Best: after city lights switch onImage source ↗
Shopping

南京路步行街

Nanjing Road

Shanghai’s best-known pedestrian shopping street mixes historic department stores, bright signs, food shops and heavy foot traffic. It works especially well as a walking link from People’s Square to the Bund.

Allow: 1–2 hoursImage source ↗
Modern Shanghai

陆家嘴

Lujiazui

Shanghai’s futuristic financial district is home to the Oriental Pearl Tower, Shanghai Tower and a striking elevated pedestrian network. Visit an observation deck or enjoy the towers from the riverfront.

Best: late afternoon into eveningImage source ↗
City walk

原法租界街区

Former French Concession

Leafy streets, garden villas and Art Deco apartment buildings give this area a more intimate character. Wukang Road, Hunan Road and Fuxing West Road form an excellent half-day walk.

Best: weekday morningImage source ↗

Old Shanghai

Classical garden design, folk religion and a bustling commercial quarter meet in the heart of the old city.

Classical garden

豫园

Yu Garden

A compact Ming-style garden of pavilions, ponds, rockeries and winding corridors. Its carefully framed views reward a slow visit, especially before the surrounding bazaar reaches peak crowd levels.

Allow: 1.5–2 hoursImage source ↗
Temple & tradition

城隍庙

City God Temple

The historic Daoist temple sits within a crowded old-city precinct of lanterns, snack shops and traditional-style buildings. The religious complex itself is smaller and calmer than the surrounding commercial bazaar.

Combine with Yu GardenImage source ↗

Play & Escape

Choose a full-scale theme park day or trade central Shanghai for the bridges and canals of an old water town.

Full day

上海迪士尼

Shanghai Disneyland

A destination-scale park known for its enormous Enchanted Storybook Castle, TRON Lightcycle Power Run, Pirates of the Caribbean and the Zootopia land. Arrive before opening and reserve the entire day.

Allow: 1 full dayImage source ↗
Water town

朱家角古镇

Zhujiajiao

An accessible water town on Shanghai’s western edge, with stone bridges, narrow lanes and boat rides along canals lined by Ming- and Qing-era houses. It is atmospheric but overlaps somewhat with Suzhou’s canal districts.

Allow: half to full dayImage source ↗

Suzhou Gardens

These spaces turn water, stone, plants and architecture into miniature landscapes designed to unfold one view at a time.

UNESCO garden

苏州拙政园

Humble Administrator’s Garden

Suzhou’s largest and most celebrated classical garden uses broad ponds, islands, pavilions and changing sightlines to create a landscape that feels much larger than its actual footprint.

Best: at opening timeImage source ↗
I. M. Pei

苏州博物馆

Suzhou Museum

I. M. Pei’s elegant museum fuses modern geometry with Suzhou’s white walls, dark roofs, courtyards and water. Inside, highlights include local archaeology, Wu-school painting and Buddhist treasures from ancient pagodas.

Allow: 1.5–2 hoursImage source ↗
UNESCO garden

苏州留园

Lingering Garden

More intimate than the Humble Administrator’s Garden, the Lingering Garden is admired for its limestone rockeries, covered corridors, framed views and beautifully balanced central pond.

Allow: 1.5–2 hoursImage source ↗

Canals & Old Streets

White walls, dark roof tiles, stone bridges and evening lanterns reveal Suzhou’s living relationship with water.

Historic district

平江路历史街区

Pingjiang Road

A stone-paved lane follows a narrow canal past old houses, teahouses and small bridges. Step into the side alleys for a quieter atmosphere, or pause for a traditional Suzhou pingtan performance.

Best: morning or late afternoonImage source ↗
Daytime

山塘街

Shantang Street by Day

Built along a historic canal leading toward Tiger Hill, Shantang Street combines whitewashed waterside houses, arched bridges, shops and boat rides. Daylight makes its architectural details easier to appreciate.

Allow: 1.5–2 hoursImage source ↗
Night

山塘街夜景

Shantang Street at Night

After sunset, red lanterns and shop lights reflect across the canal while boats pass beneath stone bridges. It is the street’s most photogenic period—and also its busiest.

Best: shortly after sunsetImage source ↗
